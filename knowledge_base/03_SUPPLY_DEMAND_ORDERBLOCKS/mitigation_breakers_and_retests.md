## What is it?

Mitigation is used in the referenced teaching as a possible unwinding or
adjustment of positions when price revisits a former trading area. That
is an explanatory hypothesis, not an observation of anyone's account.
A retest is the measurable return to a predeclared reference after a
later event. A breaker is not simply a new name applied to every rectangle
that fails: the referenced lesson discusses an older extreme, an intervening
swing, a run beyond the extreme, a structural break and a subsequent return.

In the **Mitigation Blocks** caption track, the passage around 2:38–5:18
describes a failed swing, a break of the internal reference and attention
to a prior candle near that reference. Around 6:38, the speaker explicitly
leaves uncertainty about whether the earlier positions actually need
unwinding. The later candle-range discussion is around 13:26–14:31. This
is a limited attributed account of the teaching, not proof that identifiable
participants held the positions described.

In the **ICT Breaker Block** track, passages around 0:35–2:31 and 4:59–7:09
distinguish a run beyond an old extreme from the subsequent break of
the intervening swing and later return. The bullish and bearish constructions
mirror their direction; the candle-selection discussion is around 7:42–9:16.
The speaker's claims about stops and trapped traders remain interpretation,

### Original three-swing audit

Hoshi's independent diagnostic is
`hoshi-confirmed-three-swing-role-retest-1`. It separates two price-shape
hypotheses without claiming to implement the complete lectures. For an
upward role-change question, take the latest three known alternating
pivots: low, intervening high, later low. For a downward question, use
high, intervening low, later high. All must be strict two-left/two-right
pivots confirmed before the break candle opens, and their origin times
must increase strictly. Same-bar dual extrema and nonalternating triples
are ineligible under this version.

Compare the first and final extrema. For the upward case, a lower final
low is a swept-extreme shape; a higher final low is a failed-downswing
shape. For the downward case, a higher final high is swept-extreme and
a lower final high is failed-upswing. Equal extrema are ambiguous here
and rejected. These classifications describe prices only. A lower low
does not prove that a real sell stop was triggered, nor a higher high
that a particular trader became trapped.

The event must first close beyond the intervening pivot in the proposed
new direction, with the preceding close on the original side. No earlier
close after that reference's confirmation may already have crossed it.
This prevents a later recross from being counted as the initial role
change. The feature becomes known at the event close, never at the
old origin or an unconfirmed last swing.

For a deterministic zone, this original method selects the latest up
candle at or within two bars before the intervening high for an upward
case, or the latest down candle similarly near the intervening low for
a downward case. It must lie strictly after the first pivot. Use its
full high-low range. If no such candle exists, the method has no zone;
it does not search arbitrary older candles until a future reaction fits.
This bounded selection differs from discretionary or proprietary variants.

The new upward reference is studied as possible demand and the downward
reference as possible supply. Exactly twenty completed bars after the
break are observed for contact, favourable closing rejection and wrong-side
wick or close. The first invalidating close ends the active lifecycle.
Contact on an invalidating candle does not become a held rejection. A
missing twenty-bar horizon remains null, not a shorter favourable window.

## Why does it form?

The observable sequence is a local alternation, a subsequent reference
break and possibly a later return. A role-reversal interpretation can
be useful for organizing those prices. It is not the only explanation
for a reaction: new valuations, changing liquidity, fresh orders or
unrelated participants can generate similar geometry. Without position
or order records, claims about who is reducing losses remain uncertain.

The first versus final extreme comparison distinguishes two hypotheses
with different histories. A failed attempt to reach an earlier extreme
does not contain the same geometric event as an actual excursion beyond
it. Requiring the latter for one research family while allowing the former
for another makes the distinction falsifiable. It does not establish
that either family has superior expectancy.

Retest language also mixes location and behaviour unless carefully defined.
A bar can intersect a former area, close inside it, reject away from it,
or pass entirely through it. Calling all four mitigation conceals the
outcome. The diagnostic stores these events separately. It also preserves
a favourable early rejection followed by later failure, rather than
allowing the first attractive candle to determine the entire label.

## Application zone

Apply the distinction when comparing a failed-swing hypothesis with a
swept-extreme role-change hypothesis. Freeze the anchors and method before
looking forward. Keep their actual confirmation times and disclose the
timeframe. A local shift can coexist with opposing larger-frame structure;
that conflict needs separate evidence rather than a new name for the
same small pattern.

The primary captions resolve what the referenced recordings discuss, but
they do not independently verify their historical charts. In particular,
the narration's numerical chart illustrations lack a sufficiently verified
instrument/date/venue record here. They are not used as the required real
historical cases. The three examples below instead use independently
retained source prices and the explicitly original Hoshi protocol.

Those prices are **Binance Spot BTCUSDT**, quoted in USDT per BTC, with
daily bars from 00:00:00 to 23:59:59.999 UTC. The existing bounded captures
were retrieved during the 28 October 2026 source review and already used
for other chapters. They are not an unseen holdout or a guarantee of
original-day data vintage. The zone receipt references exact raw-response
hashes and separates origin, pivot confirmation, break and outcome times.

Before considering a paper opportunity, assess width and invalidation
relative to observable costs and cash risk. The same chosen candle may
be much wider than a narrow pivot wick or three-bar gap. Those are
different reference objects, with different contact and failure frequencies.
Do not switch among them after seeing which one would have produced
the most attractive historical entry.

## Failure zones

No retest pattern works 100%. A well-ordered break can move away without
returning, so a waiting zone-entry model has no opportunity. A return
can initially reject and later violate the boundary. A first contact
can itself be the failure. Those possibilities must survive the ledger
even when the broad directional story appears persuasive.

Confirmation delay can remove opportunities. Requiring two right-hand
bars means the final extremum is unavailable on its origin day. This
audit also waits for the later close beyond the intervening reference.
It therefore cannot claim a retrospective fill at the origin's best
price. A discretionary earlier hypothesis would be a different model
and must accept the information it lacked at that earlier time.

A closing invalidation rule is not a stop-fill guarantee. A wick can
move beyond a boundary well before the daily close, and a position
model may need to act earlier. Daily OHLC also omits order arrival,
queue, spread and intrabar execution sequence. A source-verified candle
does not certify an executed trade or the amount of risk experienced.

Captions impose another limit: recognizable speech errors and unviewed
charts can make exact candle-selection details uncertain. Only the limited
textual distinctions above are attributed to the recordings. Hoshi's
numeric protocol remains original, with `creator_parity_validated: false`,
unknown actual stops and null win probability. The separate study PDF

## Three real historical examples with dates

### Example 1 — 19 January–16 February 2020: swept-extreme shape without a later zone contact

The three eligible pivots are a **19 January** low at **8,466**, a
**22 January** high at **8,818** and a **24 January** low at **8,238**.
The final low is lower than the first, making this an upward swept-extreme
shape under the original rule. All three are confirmed before **27 January**
opens. That day's close **8,907.57** first crosses the intervening high.

The selected original-rule candle is **21 January**, an up candle with
range **8,488–8,789**. That interval becomes a role-change reference only
at the 27 January closing break. Across twenty later bars, **28 January–
16 February**, no bar contacts it. There is no closing rejection or
invalidation either. A story about an attractive former resistance area
cannot turn the absence of a return into a filled long at that area.

### Example 2 — 4–30 April 2021: an early closing rejection followed by invalidation

The eligible low/high/low sequence is **4 April, 56,388**; **6 April,
59,495.24**; and **7 April, 55,473**. The last low is lower, and its
confirmation completes at the 9 April close. The **10 April** close
**59,769.13** first crosses the intervening high after those confirmations.
The original-rule up origin is **5 April**, range **56,777.77–59,272**.

On **11 April**, low **59,232.52** enters the interval and close
**60,002.43** finishes above it. That is a genuine first contact and
favourable closing rejection under the protocol. On **18 April**, however,
low **50,931.30** and close **56,150.01** breach its lower boundary.
The twenty-bar observation runs through **30 April**, retaining the
earlier rejection and later invalidation. Neither is erased to manufacture
a single winning-breaker label, and no fill or account return is inferred.

### Example 3 — 24 August–26 September 2022: failed-upswing variant invalidates on its first contact

The eligible high/low/high sequence is **24 August, 21,900**;
**28 August, 19,520**; and **30 August, 20,576.25**. Here the final high
does not exceed the first: this is the failed-upswing variant, rather
than a swept-extreme variant. The **6 September** close **18,790.61**
first crosses the intervening low. The selected down origin is the
28 August candle, range **19,520–20,171.18**.

The first later contact occurs on **9 September**, with high **21,597.22**
and close **21,360.11**. It both intersects the interval and closes
above its upper boundary, invalidating the new supply hypothesis. There
is no favourable closing rejection before that failure. The fixed
observation ends **26 September**; the active lifecycle already ended
on the ninth. An orderly failed swing therefore does not guarantee
that former support will hold as resistance.

## How to use it to HUNT a trade

First decide which hypothesis is being studied and freeze its reference
policy. Distinguish a failed swing from an actual excursion beyond an
old extreme, and distinguish both from known stop executions. Require
all chosen pivots to be available at the stated cutoff. If the candle
selection cannot be reproduced, record the uncertainty rather than
claiming a certified creator setup.

Then observe a genuinely later return with an explicit expiry and failure
rule. Preserve no-contact, contact-without-rejection, early rejection,
same-bar invalidation and missing-outcome states. An older origin date
may explain location but never replaces the later activation time.
Do not promote a touch to a limit fill or infer actual trapped inventory
from a lower low or higher high.

A complete paper strategy still needs independent context, supported
current data, observed executable prices, realistic costs, bounded risk
and an exit/ambiguity policy. Evaluate chronologically on independent
data before making performance claims. Evidence coverage is not win
probability; missing essential inputs mean WAIT and probability remains
null until independently calibrated.
