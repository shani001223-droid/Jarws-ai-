# SMC evidence and counterexamples: complete ledgers before persuasive stories

Reviewed: 28 October 2026, Asia/Karachi. Scope: testing the limited price
sequence defined in this curriculum, retaining absent, rejected and adverse
observations. These are exploratory source measurements, not independently
evaluated strategy returns, model training or delivered runtime V3 replay.

## What is it?

Evidence is a record that permits a claim to be checked, including what
would contradict it. For a chart-based SMC hypothesis, that record needs
the exact instrument and venue, source observations, information cutoff,
feature definition, candidate denominator and later outcome rule. A selected
winning screenshot can illustrate a construction but cannot establish how
often it occurred or whether it was profitable after execution costs.

Separate recognition evidence from predictive evidence. Recognition asks
whether the stated conditions existed using information available then.
Predictive evaluation asks whether a predeclared future outcome or strategy
behaviour improves on an appropriate comparison under realistic constraints.
A detector can pass recognition tests while producing no usable opportunities
or adverse future paths. Correctly recognizing a past event is not a
calibrated estimate of a future win probability.

A counterexample should match the claim being tested. A formation without
a later retest contradicts “every formation provides this entry,” but does
not by itself contradict a model that explicitly requires a retest. A first
contact that fills the gap contradicts a claimed held, unfilled-gap retest.
A later rebound can contradict uninterrupted directional continuation,
while remaining compatible with some separately specified shorter exit.
Changing the claim after each outcome makes the method unfalsifiable.

### The bounded exploratory ledger

The original offline study `hoshi-exploratory-sequence-ledger-1` evaluates
`hoshi-sweep-break-gap-audit-1` on every eligible confirmation date C in
each existing capture. The first twenty-one bars provide minimum warmup;
every later date is examined in both directions. These are directional
decision opportunities in a diagnostic scan, not independent trades.
The same price history supports both directions.

All qualified displacement/origin/gap formations enter the exported ledger,
including those rejected for missing sweep or fresh break. For each row,
the study retains direction, origin date, stage dates, break label, missing
conditions, price-chain qualification, horizon status, geometric retest,
invalidation and a raw five-bar close change when available. It does not
discard a row because the subsequent direction is inconvenient.

The study also counts decisions with an incomplete five-later-bar horizon.
An incomplete outcome is null, not a zero change. The actual selected
captures have no qualified chain in their final incomplete tails, but
that observed absence must not become a general assumption. A regression
using an earlier real-data prefix confirms that a qualified tail chain
keeps its missing outcome.

## Why does it form?

Apparent pattern reliability can arise from selection as well as from any
economic relationship. An analyst can choose swing radius, tolerated
equality, sweep lookback, displacement multiplier, origin, retest rule and
holding horizon. Looking at outcomes before choosing among them creates
many opportunities to fit historical noise. A polished final rule hides
those discarded choices unless the research process records them.

The checked author-hosted paper by Bailey, Borwein, López de Prado and Zhu,
dated 27 February 2015, examines backtest selection and proposes a framework
for assessing overfitting, including CSCV. Its warning concerns selection
across configurations, not a formula for certifying these eight price chains.
No PBO, CSCV or performance statistic from that paper is calculated here.
Independent chronological evaluation and a record of trials are still
required; naming a statistical method is not implementing it.

Dependence creates another source of apparent replication. Nearby C dates
can reuse the same origin and substantially overlapping future bars. Two
different formations may therefore be descriptions of one move. Likewise,
structure, displacement and a gap can all arise from the same impulse.
Adding their counts or percentages does not create more independent
evidence than the underlying observations provide.

Narrative flexibility can further conceal failures. A rising move after
a local premium label may be renamed expansion; a falling move after
discount may be assigned a newly enlarged range. These may be legitimate
new descriptions at later times, but they cannot repair the earlier
frozen prediction. Keep the original version and record the later change
as a new observation.

## Application zone

Use a ledger when a research question needs denominators rather than
examples alone. The captures here are **Binance Spot BTCUSDT**, USDT per
BTC, daily UTC 00:00:00–23:59:59.999. They contain 182, 184 and 245 bars,
respectively: 611 in total. They were retrieved during the 28 October 2026
review and already inspected in earlier curriculum work. They are explicitly
not unseen holdouts or independently preserved original-day vintages.

With twenty-one warmup bars removed from each window, there are 548 eligible
C dates and **1,096 directional decisions**. Of those, 1,066 have a complete
five-later-bar horizon and 30 have an incomplete horizon. There are **44
formations**, but only **eight** satisfy the narrower ordered price chain.
Among those eight complete chains, two have an eligible geometric retest,
one invalidates before such a retest and five have no eligible retest
within the five-bar window.

These are event-category counts, not wins and losses. Both geometric events
still lack full strategy evidence and actual execution inputs. Every audit
decision remains WAIT. No orders were submitted, while simulated trade
count, strategy PnL, drawdown and win probability remain null because this
tool is not a trade simulator. Null drawdown must not be advertised as
zero drawdown, and no submitted orders must not be advertised as a
demonstrated profitable zero-trade strategy.

## Failure zones

No SMC pattern works 100%. A high coverage score can mean many required
features are present while providing no calibrated probability. Conversely,
a rejected chain can precede a large favourable move. That observation
does not entitle an analyst to change the entry retrospectively. A method
is evaluated by its own executable rules, not by all movement visible
after its candidate appeared.

Costs and payoff shape matter separately from recognition. In a purely
hypothetical model paying 2R for a win and losing 1R otherwise, with a
fixed additional cost cR per trade, expected payoff is (3p − 1 − c)R.
The algebraic break-even probability is (1 + c) / 3. If the invented
illustrative cost is 0.1R, that becomes approximately 36.67%. This is
original arithmetic under chosen assumptions, not a measured Hoshi win
rate, actual fee tier or valid payoff model for gapped executions.

A cost-free raw endpoint cannot supply that p. It omits entry timing,
spread, partial fills, exits and path dependence. The SEC's checked stock
order bulletin explains price and execution constraints, including the
distinction between a stop trigger and its actual fill. Those principles
do not establish Binance historical fills, and a daily candle cannot
resolve every same-bar order sequence.

Overlaps weaken simple sample-size claims. The 2020 formation ledger has
five pairs of C dates whose hypothetical five-bar future windows overlap,
and the 2022 ledger has four. Two 2020 origin/direction groups are reused
by consecutive formations. These counts do not establish the exact
effective sample size, but they refute treating all 44 formations as
independent trials. A precise independent evaluation must also address
overlapping positions and aggregate exposure.

## Three real historical examples with dates

### Example 1 — January–June 2020: no geometric retest despite a large observed move

The **1 January–30 June 2020** capture contains 182 daily bars. After warmup,
322 directional decisions yield 17 formations and two ordered price chains:
**28 January**, long, and **9 March**, short. Neither has an eligible
gap retest within the following five bars. There is therefore no geometric
entry event under this retest rule in this bounded window.

The 9 March chain's five-bar raw close change is approximately **−34.7775%**
through **14 March**. That large move is a genuine price observation, but
the waiting retest rule did not acquire an eligible entry. Claiming it
as a captured short would replace the entry model after seeing the decline.
The ledger also retains the **29 January** formation rejected for lacking
a fresh reference break, even though its origin and much of its surrounding
geometry resemble the preceding day's formation.

The window has five overlapping formation-horizon pairs and two reused
origin groups: 25 January across 28/29 January confirmations, and 23 February
across 26/27 February confirmations. Those repetitions show why a catalogue
of many rectangles can exaggerate the number of distinct opportunities.

### Example 2 — March–August 2021: favourable movement does not convert absent entries into trades

The **1 March–31 August 2021** capture contains 184 bars and 326 directional
decisions. Ten formations produce three price chains, confirmed **13 May**,
**24 July** and **7 August**. Only the 13 May bearish chain has a geometric
retest, on **14 May**. The two bullish chains do not acquire one in their
fixed five-bar windows. There are no overlapping five-bar formation-horizon
pairs under this study's definition in this particular capture.

The 24 July chain nevertheless has a raw five-bar close increase of about
**16.8087%** through **29 July**. That is a clear example of a favourable
directional path without the declared retest entry. The 7 August chain's
corresponding endpoint through **12 August** is about **−0.3893%**, despite
its bullish chain qualification. Both are retained, and neither becomes
account PnL. The 14 May geometric event also remains WAIT because joint
block overlap and the other full strategy inputs were not established.

### Example 3 — May–December 2022: invalidation and an adverse bearish endpoint both survive the ledger

The **1 May–31 December 2022** capture contains 245 bars and 448 directional
decisions. Seventeen formations yield three price chains: **31 May**, long;
**9 November**, short; and **17 December**, short. The May chain invalidates
on **1 June**, its first gap contact, before an eligible held retest.
The November chain gets no retest in its five-bar window. The December
chain records a geometric retest on **18 December**.

The 9 November bearish chain has a raw close change of approximately
**+4.3752%** through **14 November**, opposite to its indicated study direction.
The 17 December bearish chain's raw endpoint through **22 December** is
approximately **+0.2677%**. A geometric retest is therefore not synonymous
with a favourable five-bar close, still less a profitable short. Four
formation-horizon pairs overlap in this capture; missing strategy, cost
and fill evidence prevents a calibrated performance claim.

## How to use it to HUNT a trade

Start by stating the claim and its possible failure before selecting charts.
Freeze feature versions, source clocks, candidate universe, eligibility,
holding policy and cost/ambiguity assumptions. Record all inspected trials
and all candidate outcomes, including absent and rejected chains. Retain
the original source hashes so an attractive later annotation can be
compared with what was actually available.

Next separate exploratory discovery from independent evaluation. These
already inspected windows can support debugging, teaching and falsification
of overbroad claims. They cannot be renamed a holdout. A later release
evaluation needs versioned point-in-time history/global-flow inputs,
chronological partitions, realistic execution costs, gap/ordering handling,
portfolio drawdown and exportable evidence. Additional samples must not
be chosen only because the current rule looks good there.

Report the meaning of every number. Event counts are not trade counts;
raw price changes are not account returns; missing outcomes are not zeros;
and evidence coverage is not probability. If no eligible events occur,
report that result and the window rather than claiming perfect accuracy.
If the evidence lacks current or essential inputs, the operational answer
is WAIT with probability null.
