# Inversion FVG and balanced ranges: new hypotheses after old ones fail

## What is it?

An inversion FVG is a proposed change of role for an earlier gap area after
price has invalidated its original directional interpretation. It does not
erase the original failure. The old candidate and the new opposite hypothesis
need distinct activation times, prerequisites and invalidation rules. A
reversal observed later cannot be assigned to the original gap's formation
time as though it were known then.

The checked original **16 September 2024 FVG lesson**, publicly uploaded
15:48:45 UTC, discusses a potential opposite-direction gap interpretation
after a loss around 32:54–33:09 and 52:33–52:48. Around 23:45–24:33 it
also discusses a balanced range between gaps and qualifies boundaries using
body-gap information. These are limited caption attributions. Statements
about recovering a loss are not adopted as a promise, risk policy or measured
probability. A stopped candidate does not guarantee its opposite will work.

Hoshi's original inversion convention is **hoshi-first-close-failure-inversion-1**.
For a bullish outer-wick gap, the first later completed close strictly below
its lower boundary activates a hypothetical bearish role. For a bearish gap,
the first close strictly above its upper boundary activates a hypothetical
bullish role. The search is bounded to twenty subsequent minute bars. A wick
touch alone does not meet this new closing-failure rule, even though it can
retire the original candidate under the preceding chapter's stricter wick rule.

The balanced-range diagnostic is **hoshi-first-opposite-wick-gap-overlap-1**.
It finds the first later opposite-direction strict gap within twenty bars
that has a positive-width intersection with the parent gap. If their intervals
are [L1, H1] and [L2, H2], the overlap is **[max(L1,L2), min(H1,H2)]**,
provided the lower endpoint is strictly below the upper. A single shared
boundary has zero width and does not qualify. This transparent geometry is
not certified equivalence with every discretionary creator use of balanced
price range.

## Why does it form?

Price can traverse the same region in opposite directions as conditions change.
A previously useful directional reference may then be revisited from the other
side. That observation motivates a role-change hypothesis, but does not show
that an invisible order balance has been repaired. The observed path can also
reflect ordinary variation, a changing quote environment or new information.
Candles do not identify participants, inventory or coordinated intention.

An apparent overlap can arise from the choice of sampling intervals. Two
opposite three-candle patterns may even share a forming candle. Their signals
are therefore dependent descriptions of nearby transactions, not two independent
votes. Increasing the number of labels on the same price history cannot justify
increasing a confidence percentage as though fresh evidence were added.

Role changes also create an easy hindsight trap. After a long hypothesis fails,
it is tempting to describe the entire earlier area as bearish from the start.
That suppresses the first loss and gives the later idea information too soon.
The failure close is an additional event. Before that event, the new inversion
does not exist under this version, regardless of how obvious the completed
chart looks afterward.

A balanced interval is not inherently directional. Price can close above it,
below it or oscillate through it. Its midpoint is arithmetic, not an equilibrium
price established by a complete auction. Selecting a direction requires a
separate frozen hypothesis. Hoshi's overlap receipt leaves directional bias
null rather than borrowing the direction of the later candle or eventual move.

## Application zone

First freeze the parent FVG from completed A/B/C bars using the prior chapter's
strict outer-wick convention. Store its original formation time and lifecycle.
For the inversion search, inspect later bars chronologically and stop at the
first strict closing failure, never the failure that later produced the best
retest. If twenty complete bars pass without one, record no closing failure
within that horizon. If the capture ends earlier, record incomplete observation,
not a completed absence or a failed trade.

At the failure close, create a new feature with the old bounds, opposite
direction and earliest possible post-close availability. Provider arrival
remains unknown. The failure bar cannot simultaneously serve as a later
confirmed inversion retest. It helped define the new hypothesis; its earlier
intrabar path was unavailable under that completed-close rule. Retest analysis
starts with the next bar.

Observe five subsequent minute bars for the new inversion. Contact means
overlap with the old interval. A close holding the midpoint in the proposed
new direction is a geometric contact/hold. A close beyond the opposite far
boundary invalidates the inversion before any same-bar hold can be counted.
This is an explicitly different closing-invalidation policy from the old
candidate's wick retirement. Neither policy can be chosen after seeing which
would rescue the example.

For balanced ranges, require both gaps to have completed. The feature is known
only after the later C closes. Keep both original gap records, the intersection
and whether their forming bars overlap. Do not pretend both parent candidates
are still active: the intersection can remain a historical reference even if
the earlier directional gap already retired. Historical reference geometry
does not reactivate a canceled order or prove fresh resting liquidity.

Its following five bars record contact and closes on either side without
assigning an automatic direction or PnL. An entry model using the overlap
would still need context, a defined permitted side, a reachable post-decision
price, cost/risk limits and cancellation. Missing global-flow, source freshness
or execution evidence remains missing. Every diagnostic decision is WAIT;
win probability is null and real orders are disabled.

## Failure zones

**Immediate reversal assumed:** getting stopped does not prove the opposite
direction has an edge. An inversion can show an early closing hold and fail
minutes later. Increasing size or chasing to recover the earlier loss changes
the risk process. A new candidate must fit the same bounded loss and exposure
limits independently of emotional attachment to the failed one.

**A wick substituted for a close:** the old candidate can retire under a wick
threshold while the new closing-failure threshold remains unmet. Those states
are compatible. Calling any stop hit a fully confirmed inversion silently
merges two rules. Equality at the closing boundary is also not a strict
failure in this convention.

**Failure-bar hindsight:** a large failure candle may already cross the zone
in both directions. Using that same bar to certify a later retest gives the
new hypothesis access before its activation. Prefix tests explicitly require
that inversion features be absent before the failure closes and stable afterward.
Subsequent outcome bars cannot change the selected failure.

**False balanced-range completeness:** the checked 2024 commentary includes
boundary nuance that a simple wick intersection does not reproduce. The
chapter provides an original testable approximation with parity false. It
does not claim that every overlapping gap is the creator's valid setup or
that body-based refinements were silently implemented.

**Shared evidence counted twice:** two gaps can share bars and their retest
windows can overlap. A future scoring system must not treat their intersection,
midpoint, inversion and contact as independent statistical confirmations.
They may still be separate descriptive fields, but their dependency is part
of the evidence, not something hidden by a larger score.

**Boundless searching:** a twenty-bar absence is not absence forever. Extending
the horizon until an opposite gap appears changes the experiment. Conversely,
a later valid opposite candidate does not justify deleting the earlier null
result. These inspected examples are explanatory cases, not an untouched
performance sample, and no win rate is estimated.

## Three real historical examples with dates

All observations reuse the genuine **Binance Spot BTCUSDT one-minute captures**
in the source receipt. Prices are USDT per BTC and all times below UTC.
The data were retrieved on **28 October 2026**; earliest feature times are
logical completed-bar limits, not evidence the method ran prospectively in
2023/2024 or that source messages arrived without delay. These are original
retrospective diagnostics, not the creator's chart trades.

### Example 1 — 15 May 2023: inversion holds briefly, then fails too

The first parent bearish gap is **27,488.58–27,495.50**, formed by C opening
**06:32 UTC**. At **06:33**, the next candle reaches **27,498.48** and
closes **27,498.47**, above the upper boundary. Its close activates a new
long-side inversion no earlier than **06:34**. The old bearish retirement
remains recorded; it is not converted into a successful short.

The **06:34** candle contacts the area but closes **27,491.53**, just below
midpoint **27,492.04**. The **06:35** candle closes **27,503.75**, providing
a later contact/hold. Then **06:36** closes **27,479.57**, below the lower
boundary, invalidating the new long hypothesis. The fifth post-failure close
is **27,467.12**, roughly **−0.11401%** from the inversion activation candle's
close. No recovery profit or fill is inferred.

An opposite bullish gap completes at **06:35**, with overlap
**27,488.58–27,491.52** and midpoint **27,490.05**. Its forming bars share
the original parent's C candle. The next bar touches the upper overlap edge,
then the following bar closes below the overlap. This is dependent reference
geometry with an adverse later path, not independent bullish confirmation.

### Example 2 — 5 January 2024: a larger overlap still cannot guarantee support

The parent bearish gap is **43,683.73–43,736.01**, from C opening
**14:36 UTC**. The **14:37** candle closes **43,747.85**, beyond the far
boundary. The long inversion therefore becomes logically available at
**14:38**. Its first following contact closes **43,714.01**, above parent
midpoint **43,709.87**, satisfying the declared geometric hold.

The **14:39** close **43,687.00** remains above the lower boundary, but
**14:40** closes **43,641.10**, invalidating the inversion. The fifth
post-failure close is **43,285.05**, approximately **−1.05788%** from
43,747.85. A first hold did not establish durable support or a calibrated
probability. Entry and stop execution remain unknown.

The first opposite bullish gap completes at **14:39**, creating intersection
**43,683.73–43,694.80**, midpoint **43,689.265**. The following bar contacts
it, and the next closes below. No later close above this intersection is
observed in its five-bar outcome window. The original interval construction
does not turn those closes into actual order-book evidence.

### Example 3 — 8 August 2024: no inversion or overlapping opposite gap

The parent bearish gap is **59,681.63–59,708.25**, with C opening
**17:32 UTC**. The next **twenty** complete minute bars contain no close
above the upper boundary and no opposite strict gap with a positive overlap
under the declared search. Both search windows are fully observed; this is
not a truncated-data result.

The receipt therefore records **no close failure within twenty bars** and
**no opposite-gap overlap within twenty bars**, with no fabricated activation
time or inverse direction. It does not produce a zero win probability,
zero account drawdown or simulated profitable short. The later thirty-plus
minutes in the capture do not silently extend this particular search horizon.
The exact result is absence under a bounded definition.

## How to use it to HUNT a trade

Keep the failed candidate's history intact. Wait for the explicitly required
new event, then start a fresh clock and risk assessment. Require a subsequent
eligible contact and actual supported price observations. If a failure close,
opposite overlap or later entry condition is absent, export that absence and
WAIT. A historical interval is not permission to place an order at its old price.

State the boundary convention, invalidation policy, horizon and dependencies
before evaluating. Distinguish a body-qualified creator interpretation from
the original wick approximation actually implemented here. Include costs,
same-bar ambiguity, source delay and bounded paper exposure in a later causal
replay, with independent chronological validation before estimating probability.

source chapters and offline diagnostics do not constitute model training,
runtime retrieval, an execution-capable strategy or a final V3 release. No
real orders, leverage or loss-recovery promise are introduced.
