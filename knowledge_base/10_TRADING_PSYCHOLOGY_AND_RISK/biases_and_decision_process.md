# Trading biases and an auditable decision process

Source review: **28 October 2026, Asia/Karachi**. This chapter distinguishes
observable decision errors from stories about a person's motives. Its three
dated historical cases are genuine published experiments/account studies,
not invented live charts or diagnoses of individual traders. Their study
periods, publication dates and missing raw inputs are labelled separately.
This bounded curriculum is expandable; it is not every psychological finding.

## What is it?

A decision bias is a systematic departure from the stated standard of
reasoning or choice. Identifying one requires a comparison standard and
appropriate evidence. A losing trade alone does not establish a bias, and a
winning trade does not establish a sound process. Markets have uncertainty,
execution costs and structural constraints that cannot all be reduced to
psychology. HOSHI should examine the decision record rather than assert that
it knows what an investor felt.

Three classic information shortcuts concern **representativeness**—judging by
resemblance; **availability**—using what is easy to recall; and **anchoring**—
allowing a starting number to influence an estimate. Tversky and Kahneman's
1974 primary paper studies these mechanisms; a memorable resemblance is not
itself a calibrated probability. [1] For HOSHI, a war analogy or an attractive
chart screenshot is a candidate hypothesis that still needs genuine dated
inputs, counterexamples and independent evaluation.

Other useful audit categories include overconfidence, selective evidence
search, hindsight, outcome-based evaluation, recency, social imitation,
reference-price attachment and escalating commitment. These labels organize
questions; they are not an instrument that reveals a stranger's mental
state. A claim that "banks trap retail" needs independent evidence of the
specific conduct, not a bias label attached to a candle wick.

The **disposition effect** concerns a tendency to realize gains more readily
than losses, relative to the opportunities to do either. It is not equivalent
to every decision to hold a declining asset. Taxes, mandate, liquidity,
updated information and rebalancing may matter. Odean's 1998 study explicitly
compares realization opportunities rather than merely counting winning and
losing sales. [2] That denominator distinction is essential before assigning
a behavioral interpretation.

## Why does it form?

A decision maker faces a large information set with limited time and attention.
Simplifying the problem can be useful, but the simplification can discard
base rates, sample size, costs or contrary evidence. A trading interface can
make one dramatic outcome unusually salient while hiding the many ordinary
or unsuccessful cases. The resulting narrative may sound precise despite
having weak measurement behind it.

A practical example of selective evidence search is to save screenshots only
when a gap later fills. The saved collection then excludes gaps that continue,
expire, invalidate or never become tradable. The apparent success frequency
answers a question about the selected collection, not all eligible gaps.
The problem persists if every screenshot is genuine. HOSHI's earlier bounded
event ledgers therefore retain absence, overlap, censored tails and adverse
paths; those records are useful checks on an attractive story.

Reference-price attachment can make "getting back to entry" seem like a
market objective. Yet one's purchase price does not by itself change the
current information, remaining risk or alternative uses of capital. An
original process question is: if the position did not exist, would the same
exposure pass the present evidence and budget checks? That question does not
prove that selling is always correct; it makes the dependence on the old
reference explicit.

Escalating commitment arises as a decision-process concern when a rejected
hypothesis repeatedly receives more risk without a separately justified
revision. Changing a stop, adding size and rewriting the original reason
are three distinct actions that should be recorded separately. Otherwise
a later explanation can erase the very conditions under which the initial
decision failed. No increase in risk is justified merely because a series
of losses feels due to reverse.

Overconfidence also has distinct meanings. Excessive certainty about a
forecast, excessive belief in one's relative skill and excessive trading
are not identical observations. More trading can have rational motives,
so a turnover statistic alone cannot diagnose every account. The relevant
question is whether the extra decisions survive costs and an appropriate
comparison, and whether the asserted certainty matches independently
observed calibration. [3]

## Application zone

Use a decision record created **before** the relevant outcome. It should
identify the hypothesis, rule version, instrument/venue, source observation
and arrival times, supporting and conflicting evidence, invalidation,
allowed paper risk, costs, and conditions requiring WAIT. Store the record
only through an existing authorized privacy mechanism; this chapter does not
turn on user memory or create a new personal-data store.

Separate observation from inference. "The completed candle crossed a
previously confirmed high" is a measurable claim. "A particular institution
wanted to take my stop" is a claim about identity and intent that the candle
does not establish. "It resembles a selected historical case" describes a
comparison, not a causal mechanism. This separation gives HOSHI something
concrete to validate without teaching an unsolicited psychology lecture.

Before accepting an analogy, define the population it could come from.
Ask how many comparable events failed, which were omitted, and what was
known at the decision time. Check that the historical case has the same
instrument definition, session clock and execution assumptions. A 2003
headline and a current headline may share words while differing in policy,
valuation, positioning or prior expectations. Similarity must not conceal
those differences.

Apply symmetric checks to favorable and unfavorable scenarios. If price
moves toward a target, ask whether the entry was genuinely available and
whether the rule had already invalidated. If it moves adversely, preserve
the original timestamps rather than drawing a new zone retrospectively.
Changing the model after inspection creates a new version and a new
validation need. A source chapter cannot supply that validation by itself.

Evaluate process and outcomes in separate fields. A compliant paper decision
can lose; an invalid decision can win. Compare the full sequence, including
skipped and zero-trade periods, with the declared strategy and realistic
costs. Numerical probability evaluation requires explicit forecasts and
subsequent independently labelled outcomes. A rule-coverage score lacks
those properties, so this chapter does not convert HOSHI's confluence weights
into a success probability.

## Failure zones

Naming a bias after seeing a result can itself become hindsight. A label
that could explain either direction without a testable difference is not a
useful model. Likewise, saying a trader was fearful because they sold and
greedy because they held makes the claim impossible to refute. HOSHI should
record available evidence and uncertainty, not offer a confident account of
hidden emotion.

Awareness does not guarantee correction. A checklist can become ceremonial,
with every box filled from the same favored story. A more useful design
requires a specific disconfirming observation and a consequence if it occurs.
That consequence could be suspending a paper candidate, expiring stale
information or opening a new research version. It is not a promise that a
human or model will follow the process perfectly.

Avoid confusing independence with presentation. Several indicators calculated
from the same closing prices are not several independent witnesses. Many
social accounts reposting one original source do not create independent
confirmation. A historical anecdote repeated across websites remains one
anecdote. The evidence record needs provenance and dependence, not only a
count of agreeing descriptions.

The historical research below has limited transferability. Older brokerage
fees, sample selection and available securities differ from present
single-venue crypto paper research. A laboratory estimate is not a live
market-price response. No participant-level replication or raw broker data
was retrieved here. Source findings do not identify the user as biased,
prove institutional intent or establish the profitability of an automatic
counter-bias strategy.

## Three real historical examples with dates

### Example 1 — 27 September 1974: arbitrary anchors in published judgment research

Tversky and Kahneman's *Science* paper was published **27 September 1974**.
In its wheel-of-fortune example, groups received starting numbers **10**
or **65**, then estimated the percentage of African countries in the United
Nations. The reported median estimates were **25% and 45%**, respectively.
These are experimental responses reported in the paper, not a measurement
of actual UN membership, stock returns or trades. The publication date does
not supply exact collection dates for each participant's response. [1]

Original HOSHI application: an arbitrary prior entry, a round chart level or
a displayed target can serve as a starting reference in a later judgment.
A robust process should require evidence for the estimate independently of
that display. For example, ask which confirmed observation defines a range
before computing premium/discount; do not choose boundaries only because
they place today's price in the desired half.

The experiment does not establish the size of an anchoring effect in current
BTC prices, prove that every user anchors, or identify a tradable reversal.
A market adaptation would need its own predeclared test and observations.
No historical chart, venue, quote or execution outcome is implied by the
laboratory case. The useful lesson is to audit the reference's origin rather
than treat a vivid number as evidence.

### Example 2 — January 1987–December 1993 accounts, published October 1998

Odean's *Are Investors Reluctant to Realize Their Losses?* appeared in the
**October 1998** *Journal of Finance*. It examines **10,000 discount-broker
accounts** selected from accounts active in 1987, using trade records from
**January 1987 through December 1993**. Its reported aggregate preference
for realizing gains is evaluated relative to available gain/loss opportunities.
These are historical account observations, not a real-time signal released
during the sample. [2]

Original analysis: opportunity-adjusted denominators prevent a rising market
from being mistaken automatically for a preference to sell winners. In a
HOSHI process review, counts of successful-looking exits also need the
corresponding opportunities, exposures and rejected decisions. Merely listing
three profitable sales cannot show that the exit rule outperformed a
consistent alternative.

The source notes incomplete views of total investor portfolios and possible
survivorship effects from closed accounts not being replaced. Raw customer
records and their full execution clocks are not available in this curriculum.
We therefore do not re-estimate the study, assign a psychological diagnosis
to an individual account, or infer a present-day win rate. Holding a loser
can be assessed against the predeclared hypothesis and risk constraints;
the purchase-price label alone is not a sufficient judgment.

### Example 3 — 1991–1996 brokerage sample, published April 2000

Barber and Odean's *Trading Is Hazardous to Your Wealth* appeared in
**April 2000**. Of a broader brokerage sample, **66,465 households** held
individual common stocks during at least one month. The methods distinguish
trade records from **January 1991 through November 1996** and calculated
return observations from **February 1991 through January 1997**, based on
month-end position statements. These exact windows are more informative
than treating the title's publication year as the trading period. [3]

The paper reports lower net performance among the highest-turnover group;
that is a source finding, not HOSHI's own replication or proof that every
additional trade is harmful. Original process implication: every extra
candidate should have a reason that survives marginal costs and a defined
comparison. Increased activity, fast chart interaction or a streak of
recent wins cannot substitute for that comparison.

The study concerns historical US common-stock brokerage holdings, not one
modern crypto venue. Monthly return analysis does not provide a synchronized
intraday quote series or a live execution model. Its aggregate evidence and
theoretical interpretation do not establish every household's motive. No
raw household data, contemporary fee schedule or independently calibrated
Hoshi probability was obtained. A missing current price or failed setup
must still lead to WAIT, rather than trading more frequently to appear
active.

## How to use it to HUNT a trade

Start with the same supported-market and time-validity gates used elsewhere
in this curriculum. Record the proposed pattern and what would contradict
it before inspecting later prices. Require genuine dated evidence for each
factor; do not count an attractive narrative as a substitute for an observed
liquidity reference or current execution information. Preserve relevant
negative historical cases beside favorable ones.

Then test whether the decision changed for a defensible reason. New source
data, a completed confirming candle or an expired hypothesis can justify a
new assessment. A need to recover losses, a social-media claim or dislike of
a previous result cannot alone justify widening risk. Paper sizing remains
subject to its independent cash, cost and loss-budget controls. This chapter
adds no orders, leverage, account actions or new personal memory collection.

When explanation is not requested, concise Roman Urdu can say:
**"Evidence aur risk gates complete nahi; analogy ya recent streak se win
probability nahi banti. WAIT."** If teaching is requested, HOSHI can explain
the specific reasoning issue and its evidence without claiming to diagnose
the user. No pattern or debiasing method works 100%; historical association
is not causation, and **win_probability=null** until independent calibration.

A future decision-process evaluation should retain the original record,
revisions, actual availability times, missed decisions and costs. It must
not fabricate participant data or rebrand an educational checklist as
validated behavioral treatment. This source chapter is not model training,
a loaded runtime lesson or an implemented persistent journal. Runtime
retrieval, strategy evaluation and final integration remain separate gates.
