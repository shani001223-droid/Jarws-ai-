# War, elections and policy shocks without deterministic trade stories

Reviewed **28 October 2026, Asia/Karachi**. Three genuinely dated cases below
retain official event documents and actual daily Treasury observations.
They do not cover every war or election, describe the current tariff regime,
or establish causal announcement returns. Event dates, later official
compilations and original tradable information must remain distinct.
No unsupported 2026 event, price path or institutional intention is invented.

## What is it?

A geopolitical or policy shock is a change in information relevant to an
exposure. The relevant information can concern conflict, political authority,
trade rules, fiscal plans, financing or market access. The label alone does
not define an economic surprise. A widely anticipated announcement, a
previously unknown detail and an implementation date can have different
information content even when they concern the same policy.

A war is not one timestamp. Escalation, initial reports, official confirmation,
policy responses and subsequent developments can arrive separately across
time zones. An election similarly has voting dates, partial counts,
projections, concessions, certification and later official statistics.
A policy document has signing, publication, implementation and amendment
clocks. A later authoritative source can establish what happened without
being a valid input to an earlier trade.

The selected cases are **24 February 2022 Ukraine-related developments**,
the **8 November 2016 US general election**, and **Executive Order 14257
signed 2 April 2025**. The UN, FEC and official Federal Register/GovInfo
records support the stated historical identities. The FEC compilation
notice and Federal Register publication are later than their respective
selected market windows. That is a deliberate provenance limitation, not
permission to move their information backward in time. [1][2][3]

Historical resemblance is a research feature candidate. It is not a
calibrated probability, and "another war" is not sufficient evidence that
NAS100, gold, bonds, oil and Bitcoin must all repeat one prior direction.
The requested history weight must ultimately use versioned point-in-time
features and counterexamples, with missing inputs yielding WAIT.

## Why does it form?

Political information can affect expected cash flows, supply conditions,
inflation, financing needs, discount rates and willingness to bear risk.
Those channels need not point in the same direction. A potential supply
constraint may matter differently to a producer and a consumer. A fiscal
proposal can affect short- and long-maturity yields differently. A policy
result can reduce one uncertainty while creating another.

These are hypotheses, not measured channel contributions in this receipt.
The fact that yields or prices move near an event does not reveal how much
was caused by that event. Other releases, positioning adjustments and
changes in liquidity may occur during the same interval. A country-level
headline also need not describe a particular company's exposure accurately.
The instrument and economic link must be specified before inferring a trade.

Prior expectations are central. Election probabilities can change before
voting finishes; tariff details can have been discussed before signing;
conflict risk can have affected prices before a selected confirmation.
A pre-event price is therefore not necessarily an unaffected counterfactual.
A later official summary does not reconstruct that earlier belief state.
Without a genuine dated expectation source, surprise magnitude stays unknown.

Liquidity can also change independently of the economic thesis. A quote may
be stale, a market may be closed, or multiple participants may seek the same
exit. A correct broad interpretation can still be unusable at the proposed
size or price. HOSHI must evaluate source and execution evidence separately
rather than turn the drama of a headline into confidence.

## Application zone

Start by writing an event schema that distinguishes what is directly observed.
Store the event identifier, jurisdiction, primary source, document date,
claimed event time and its uncertainty, original receipt time and any
subsequent correction. Record a date-only source as date-only. Do not append
midnight UTC to create false precision. If a source date uses a local civil
calendar, that alone does not establish an instant on a US trading chart.

Next describe the exposure before choosing a direction. Is the instrument a
cash currency, a specific futures contract, an equity index, a bond reference
yield or spot crypto? Which hypothesized channel links the event to that
instrument, and what could contradict it? A label such as safe haven does
not replace this work. Different quote currencies, maturities and market
hours can change the observed comparison.

Build comparisons using compatible information sets. Historical features
must exclude details learned only after the decision. The event's narrative
may be used for later explanation while its first accessible source still
needs separate evidence. A certified election compilation can verify the
historical calendar; it cannot be treated as an election-night live feed.
Likewise, later legal publication does not prove that nobody knew a signed
policy earlier—it means this receipt lacks that earlier source capture.

The actual market context here is **Board H.15 DGS2/DGS10 through FRED**,
measured in **percent per annum**. Exact daily endpoints produce basis-point
changes. They do not supply an executable bond price, a current spread,
a global capital-flow measurement or an intraday announcement response.
These later-retrieved histories may differ from original vintages; original
publication and arrival times are not verified. [4][5]

A later bounded paper strategy should require fresh supported market data,
confirmed structure/liquidity and realistic risk/cost assumptions after the
necessary information is available. Missing or stale data is a reason to
withhold levels. It is not a reason to borrow a NAS100 entry from an old
chart or to substitute one venue's history for another product.

## Failure zones

The historical-story shortcut fails when it ignores prior pricing and
counterexamples. Selecting a past war in which a chosen market fell and
omitting another path creates a biased comparison. Assigning 92% confidence
because a headline sounds similar hides the absence of calibration.
Similarity should be defined before inspecting the new outcome and should
retain differences as well as matches.

Another failure is collapsing the event timeline. Voting day is not the
instant every market learned the final result. A signing date is not a
verified data-pipeline arrival time. An official statement issued during a
conflict is not necessarily the first report of the underlying action.
A replay that assigns all of these facts to the earliest date manufactures
foresight.

Window selection can manufacture a preferred narrative too. A first daily
move, a later reversal and a multi-day endpoint answer different questions.
The selected endpoint must be declared and preserved even when another
horizon looks more favorable. Weekends and missing observations must not
be filled with invented prices. A date-aligned yield change is descriptive
context, not an isolated event effect.

Legal and operational details also change. The 2025 order below is a
historical source, not a statement of present tariff obligations. A strategy
would need the applicable version and implementation information at its
own decision time. This chapter makes no current legal or account-action
recommendation. Its models cannot infer institutional intent, hidden stop
inventory or the behavior of every asset from a policy title.

## Three real historical examples with dates

### Example 1 — 24 February 2022 Ukraine-related event, 23 February–1 March yield window

The UN Ukraine statement dated **24 February 2022** describes Russian military
operations inside Ukraine and the Secretary-General's response. The selected
page establishes a dated official statement; it does not provide the first
market-news arrival timestamp, a synchronized order book or a unique global
event clock. The market window deliberately begins **23 February** and ends
**1 March**, so earlier anticipation and later developments can contribute
to the measured path. [1]

Actual DGS2 observations over the window are **1.58%, 1.54%, 1.55%, 1.44%
and 1.31%** on **23, 24, 25, 28 February and 1 March**. DGS10 is **1.99%,
1.96%, 1.97%, 1.83% and 1.72%** on those dates. Both endpoint changes are
**minus 27 basis points**. The release-date daily move and the complete
window move are different quantities; the missing weekend observations are
not imputed. [4][5]

Original analysis: this selected window contains declining Treasury reference
yields, but it does not identify a causal war effect or the returns of an
actual bond holding. It also supplies no NAS100, gold or oil execution
prices. The UN document is historical narrative evidence, not a calibrated
pattern-matching training row with a verified first-arrival timestamp.
All diagnostic trade outputs remain WAIT.

### Example 2 — 8 November 2016 US election, later FEC compilation and rising yields

The FEC's *Federal Elections 2016* compilation identifies the US general
election held **8 November 2016**. Its separate availability notice is dated
**9 January 2018**. That later notice is not a claim about the exact first
publication of every result, and certainly is not an election-night market
feed. A replay must distinguish the calendar event and contemporaneous
counts/projections from a later certified compilation. [2]

For the fixed **7–10 November 2016** window, DGS2 is **0.82%, 0.87%, 0.90%
and 0.92%** on the four daily labels. DGS10 is **1.83%, 1.88%, 2.07% and
2.15%**. The exact endpoint changes are **plus ten** and **plus 32 basis
points**. These observations describe yield levels and slope differences;
they are not equity-index returns or a reconstruction of the overnight
price path. [4][5]

Original analysis: the larger long-maturity change is a reason to preserve
instrument and horizon detail. It does not prove which fiscal, inflation,
policy or risk-expectation channel drove the move. No pre-election probability
snapshot, original result-announcement receipt, tradable prices or fills
were obtained. The selected FEC notice postdates the entire window, so the
receipt explicitly rejects its use as a point-in-time trading feature.

### Example 3 — 2 April 2025 tariff order, 7 April publication and an earlier rate window

The official record identifies **Executive Order 14257 of 2 April 2025**
and its **Federal Register publication on 7 April 2025**. This chapter uses
those historical dates and document identity, not a claim about the current
law or every later policy amendment. The selected market window is
**1–4 April 2025**, which ends **before** that Federal Register publication.
No exact first-announcement or network-ingestion timestamp was captured. [3]

DGS2 observations are **3.87%, 3.91%, 3.71% and 3.68%** on **1, 2, 3 and
4 April**, respectively. DGS10 is **4.17%, 4.20%, 4.06% and 4.01%**. Exact
endpoint changes are **minus 19** and **minus 16 basis points**. Both series
rise on the 2 April daily label relative to 1 April and then fall on later
labels. This is a daily path, not a verified announcement-time response. [4][5]

Original analysis: a source can be authoritative yet arrive too late for
the intended historical information set. The later publication must not be
backdated to authorize a 3 April trade. This does not imply that earlier
public announcements did not exist; those particular captures and arrival
clocks are missing from this receipt. No causal tariff-return estimate,
current tariff advice, executable bond price or NAS100 signal is fabricated.
The diagnostic remains WAIT.

## How to use it to HUNT a trade

Use a historical event to ask a specific, falsifiable question about a
supported instrument. Define the comparison features—such as information
novelty, prior expectations, source timing and observed market state—before
viewing the desired outcome. Do not search for a matching story only after
choosing long or short. Include mismatches and adverse examples in any
later versioned history feature set.

At decision time, require a primary or otherwise supported source, genuine
receipt time, fresh market observations and a frozen paper setup. Missing
clocks, stale headlines, unsupported instruments or absent costs lead to
WAIT with the exact gap recorded. The global-flow factor must describe
observed dated evidence, not an assumed movement caused by the headline.
No entry/SL/TP levels are produced without their own supported observations.

Keep the requested evidence weights separate from probability. A history
match may contribute to a future **rule-coverage** score only under a
predeclared definition; it cannot create a calibrated win probability from
three selected cases. Independent chronological evaluation and cost/risk
checks remain required. Concise Roman Urdu: **"War ya election ka label
akela signal nahi; source time aur actual market evidence missing ho to
WAIT."** No pattern works 100%, and **win_probability=null** until calibrated.

The adjacent `news_events_20261028.json` and
`scripts/v3_news_evidence.py` preserve the three event/document dates, six
exact H.15 captures and endpoint arithmetic. Tests reject relabelled events,
backdated later documents, forged source identities and fabricated trade
actions. These are retrospective source diagnostics, not runtime knowledge,
model training or a completed causal event-trading evaluation. Real orders,
leverage and paid data access remain disabled.
