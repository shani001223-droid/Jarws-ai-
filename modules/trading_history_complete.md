# Trading history — 1602 to the 2020–2025 period

Knowledge version: `hoshi-trading-knowledge-v2`. Historical context, not predictive alpha.

## Timeline and market impact

| Date / period | What happened | Market impact and HOSHI lesson |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1602 — Amsterdam / VOC** | Tradable shares in the Dutch East India Company helped establish a durable organized secondary share market in Amsterdam. Earlier securities and trading venues existed; avoid claiming the first financial market of any kind. | Tradable ownership connected long-term business funding to secondary-market liquidity. Distinguish a company's cash flows from its share price and the ability to exit. |
| **1637 — Tulip Mania** | Speculative Dutch tulip contracts experienced a sharp reversal in February 1637. Popular accounts exaggerate how universal the participation and macroeconomic damage were. | Expectations, leverage-like contractual exposure, thin liquidity and counterparty risk can dominate a quoted price. Do not equate every rapid rally with a historically identical bubble or invent national-collapse statistics. |
| **1902 — Dow legacy** | Charles H. Dow died in 1902 after earlier editorials on averages and trends; later writers organized the ideas now called Dow Theory. It was not one fully published 1902 strategy specification. | Trend classification and confirmation influenced technical analysis. HOSHI uses explicit closed-bar definitions; a historical label does not validate a modern trading edge. |
| **1930s — Wyckoff / Elliott** | Wyckoff's original course dates to **1931**; Elliott's *The Wave Principle* to **1938**. “1930 Elliott/Wyckoff” is a decade marker, not a shared publication year. | Price/volume and recurring-pattern frameworks expanded chart analysis. Distinguish observable bars from subjective cycle labels; avoid retrospective relabelling and look-ahead. |
| **1971 -> 1973 — monetary regime change** | Nixon suspended official dollar-gold convertibility in August 1971. Major currencies moved toward generalized floating in 1973; foreign exchange existed long before 1971. | Exchange-rate risk and macro/rate differentials became more central to currency markets and hedging. No permanent inverse dollar/crypto rule follows. |
| **1987 — Black Monday** | The Dow fell 22.6% on 19 October 1987 amid global market stress, liquidity problems and feedback involving portfolio insurance and market structure. | Liquidity can disappear precisely when needed; stop orders and normal correlation assumptions are not loss guarantees. Use stress scenarios and execution uncertainty. |
| **2008 — global financial crisis** | Housing-credit losses, leverage, funding fragility and interconnected institutions produced severe crisis; the Great Recession spans 2007–2009. | Counterparty/funding risk and forced deleveraging spread across assets. Test correlated drawdowns, data gaps and unavailable markets rather than assuming continuous fills. |
| **2009 — Bitcoin network** | The genesis block dates to 3 January 2009, following the 2008 white paper. Network creation did not immediately produce today's liquid exchange markets. | Cryptographic settlement and a 24/7 asset ecosystem emerged. Separate protocol rules from exchange custody, market fragmentation and volatile spot prices. |
| **2020–2025 — mature algorithmic / HFT era** | This is a study period of already-established algorithmic markets, not HFT's invention date. The SEC's August 2020 staff report reviews benefits, market-structure complexity and stress risks. | Automation improves some execution/liquidity conditions while introducing model, crowding, latency and operational risks. Retail cloud requests are not colocated HFT; test fees, slippage, outages and regime changes. |

## How to use this history

History helps ask better questions about liquidity, leverage, funding and
execution. It does not make OB/FVG/ICT patterns causally proven, and it does not
establish tomorrow's price. No claim of 99.999% predictive accuracy is justified.
Use primary sources for dates and qualify disputed popular stories. All effects
and “lessons” above are explanatory synthesis, not measured effect estimates.
